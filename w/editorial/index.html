02The craft

From block to cloth.

Five slow steps, all by hand, all within walking distance of our workshop.

  1. I

    Carve

    A master carver cuts each motif into seasoned sheesham wood with fine chisels. One block takes three to five days, and every colour needs its own.

  2. II

    Rekh, the outline

    The first block prints the outline in syahi, an iron black our printers ferment from rusted iron and jaggery for two weeks.

  3. III

    Datta, the colour

    Fill blocks follow, lined up by eye: madder root for the red, harda fruit for the gold. A slight shift between layers is the signature of a human hand.

  4. IV

    Dabu and indigo

    A paste of black clay, gum and wheat husk is pressed over the motifs. The cloth is dipped in the indigo vat up to seven times, and the clay cracks into fine veins.

  5. V

    Wash and sun

    Washed in running water and dried on the open ground in the sun, the colours settle and deepen. Then, and only then, we cut.

04The workshop

A courtyard in Sanganer.

Noor Siddiqui grew up two lanes away from the printing tables, where her grandfather mixed madder in copper vats. In 2014 she opened Atelier Noor with four printers and a single courtyard, and a rule she still keeps: nothing leaves the workshop until it has been worn and washed by someone on the team.

Today fourteen printers, three carvers and a small team of tailors share that courtyard. We pay above the city's skilled-wage rate, close for the monsoon, and print only what we can make well.

“A block print should look like someone made it. That is the whole idea.”Noor Siddiqui, founder
